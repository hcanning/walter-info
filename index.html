What is Walter AI?

Walter AI is Noodle Factory's intelligent AI tutor, designed to support learners 24/7 using Boston University's own course content. Instead of giving generic chatbot answers, it uses your uploaded materials, learning outcomes, and lesson structure to guide students step by step — running practice quizzes, facilitating role-play scenarios, and integrating directly into Blackboard.

Role Play

Simulate real-world conversations so students practice before it counts.

📍 Find it in: Admin Portal → left panel → Activities tab → Create Role Play
  1. Go to the Activities tab in the left panel of the Admin Portal.
  2. Click Create Role Play.
  3. Describe the AI Assistant's role and the learner's role.
  4. Write (or use AI-Assist for) a scenario description — you can link supporting documents.
  5. Set skills assessment criteria: what's being evaluated, a description, and an optional score.
  6. Optionally add a custom avatar for each role.
  7. Test it: in Learner View, type "role play," pick the scenario, and confirm to begin.
Create Role Play guide → Role Play video tutorial → Watch Role Play in Action →

Quizzes

Auto-generate quizzes and instant feedback straight from your own course content.

📍 Find it in: Admin Portal → left panel → Activities tab → Create a Quiz
  1. Go to the Activities tab in the left panel of the Admin Portal.
  2. Click Create a Quiz.
  3. Choose a quiz type: conversational, one-question-at-a-time, or show-all.
  4. Choose a question source: generate from documents, pull from a question bank, import a test, or import from an integrated source.
  5. Pick question types — multiple choice, checkbox, bullet, table, short answer, or essay.
  6. Optional: turn it into a Conversational Quiz for a back-and-forth format instead of a static test.
Build Quiz guide → Conversational Quiz guide →

Essay

Grade written responses consistently with AI-assisted, rubric-based scoring.

  1. Add an Essay question type inside a Quiz Activity (Activities tab).
  2. Turn on rubric-based grading for that question.
  3. Pick a rubric format: description-only, or a full breakdown with per-criterion max scores.
  4. Write specific, concrete rubric criteria — this is what keeps AI grading consistent.
  5. Review learner submissions and the AI's suggested feedback before releasing grades.

Deep Linking

Let instructors drop a Tutor, a specific Quiz, or a specific Role Play straight into a Blackboard page — not just one generic chat link.

What's the Activity Chooser?

When an instructor adds a "Walter AI Activity" content item in Blackboard, a picker called the Activity Chooser pops up. Instead of always linking to the general Walter AI Tutor chat, the instructor can choose exactly what that link opens:

ChoiceOpens for the student
Walter AI TutorOpen-ended tutoring/Q&A — the general chat experience.
Specific Knowledge GroupLaunches the Tutor scoped to just one Knowledge Group, so answers stay focused on that topic's material only.
Specific QuizLaunches directly into one chosen quiz, skipping any menu.
Specific Role PlayLaunches directly into one chosen role-play scenario.

General Tutor vs. Specific Knowledge Group vs. Quick Help

Inside the Activity Chooser, the Walter AI Tutor section actually offers a few different scopes to link to — pick based on how focused you want the conversation to be. Note: the top-level "General Tutor" option is temporarily missing from the Activity Chooser while our dev team restores it. It's coming back, but the guidance below already applies.

OptionWhen to use it
General TutorLinks to the full Walter AI Tutor with access to every Knowledge Group in the course. Best for one general help button — e.g. on a course homepage — when you don't want to scope students to a single topic.
Specific Knowledge GroupLinks directly into that one Knowledge Group. Best when you're embedding help on a specific module or topic page and want answers scoped to just that material, with no risk of the AI pulling in unrelated chapters. Heads up: because it's already scoped to a topic, Walter AI may start tutoring on it right away instead of sitting idle like a general chatbot waiting for a question — expected behavior, not a bug.
Quick HelpLinks to a quick chat for asking anything about the course or a topic — no lesson or Knowledge Group attached. Best when students just need to confirm or ask about information, rather than work through a full lesson.

Adding one in Blackboard

  1. (Faculty, per course) In your course, go to Build Content (Original course view) or Content Market (Ultra course view).
  2. Select Walter AI - Activity Chooser.
  3. In the Activity Chooser that appears, pick Walter AI Tutor, Specific Knowledge Group, Specific Quiz, or Specific Role Play.
  4. Save and publish — the link now sits exactly where you placed it in the course, opening exactly what you chose.

How Would Students Benefit From It?

The honest answer for when a student asks "why not just use ChatGPT?"

Students already have general AI tools, and that's fine — Walter AI isn't trying to compete with that. The difference is what it's built on and what it actually does for them:

🎯

Aligned to This Course

Answers come from the actual course materials, learning outcomes, and lesson structure — not a generic internet answer that may not match how it's taught here.

🧭

Proactively Guides

Rather than a blank chat box waiting for questions, it can point students to the right quiz, role play, or Knowledge Group — actively steering their study instead of just responding to it.

📊

Visible Progress

Learner Insights surfaces engagement and knowledge gaps, so both students and instructors can see where extra practice is actually needed.

Learner Insights

A dashboard showing how students are actually engaging — completion, quiz performance, and where the class is struggling — so the value isn't just a promise, it's visible.

Troubleshooting

The issues that come up most — usually a settings tweak, not a rebuild.

IssueWhat's happeningHow to fix it
Doesn't stick to course content / Answers are too longThe AI answers from general knowledge instead of staying inside the uploaded materials, or responses read like essays when students just wanted a quick answer.Both are persona-level tweaks (e.g. an explicit instruction like "only answer using the uploaded course materials" or "keep answers short unless asked to go deeper"), and it's not something course teams can adjust themselves from the Admin Portal — reach out to Noodle Factory and we'll tune the persona for you.
Answers before being askedStudents see Walter AI jump straight into tutoring on a topic instead of waiting quietly for a question — this isn't random, it happens when the link used was to a Specific Knowledge Group rather than General Tutor or Quick Help, so the AI is already scoped to that topic and starts guiding right away.If you want a plain, wait-for-a-question experience instead, link to general Walter AI Tutor.

What is Agent Persona?

Agent Persona is a natural-language description of how Walter AI should behave — tone, response length, boundaries, and personality. It's the first place to look whenever the AI's tone, scope, or verbosity feels off.

Boston University currently has a set of existing personas that course teams can pick from directly in the Admin Portal. Tuning or writing the actual persona instructions — tightening scope, shortening responses, or anything more custom — isn't self-service; that's done on Noodle Factory's side, so just let us know what you need.
